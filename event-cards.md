# Viral Path: Event Cards Backed by True Stories

Every event card in the spec ("Events and the reaction window") is based on one of these true stories. Team pieces stay fictional, but the cards name the real brands and people. The event alert names the story, and the **Learn more** button opens the full panel with its citation.

**What each card includes**
- **Prompt:** phone text the team can read inside the 30-second window.
- **Learn more:** the context that should change the decision, especially the target's trust going in, which the spec uses to decide flop outcomes.
- **What really happened:** the real responses, translated into the spec's moves.
- **Strategy lesson:** the principle from the strategy note that the story shows. It feeds the report-out questions.

**Moves (from the spec)**
- Flop target: **Delete · Apologize · Ignore · Lean in**
- Everyone else: **Support · Critique · Stay out**. Critique covers any public commentary at the target's expense, from a serious point to a joke.

## Strategy principles
These come from *strategy - how to make good social media ads.md*. It's an anonymous social media marketer's post on Reddit, so treat it as practitioner opinion rather than research.

| Tag | Principle | Meaning in this game |
|---|---|---|
| **S1** | Context is queen | Content has to fit the platform and what the audience expects there |
| **S2** | Conversation over vanity metrics | Treat social media as a conversation, not a billboard. Comments, shares and replies count for more than raw reach |
| **S3** | Storytelling beats promotion | People respond to real people and owned failures, and scroll past sales pitches |
| **S4** | Consistent, but experiment | Try new formats, and expect some to fail |

These map onto the spec's stats: **attention** is reach, and **trust** is whether that reach turns into value. That's the spec's third discussion question.

## Tiny Bite · Flop · Brand target · scripted for round 1
**True story:** McDonald's CEO Chris Kempczinski and the Big Arch (Mar 2026)

**Target prompt:** "Your CEO's taste-test video just went viral, for the wrong reasons. He called the burger a 'product' and took a tiny bite. Rivals are posting their own big bites. Delete, apologize, ignore, or lean in?"
**Others prompt:** "A rival's CEO taste-test is getting roasted. Support, critique, or stay out?"

**Learn more:**
- Hundreds of users filmed themselves copying the tiny bite, and reviews of the Big Arch were already lukewarm.
- Burger King's president posted a hearty Whopper bite ("Thought we'd replay this"). Wendy's posted its U.S. president eating a Baconator, a burger "worth a second bite." A&W Canada made a parody.
- McDonald's went in with decades of trust. In the spec, Lean in at High trust gains trust and costs every critiquing team 1 trust.

**What really happened:**
- **Target:** McDonald's leaned in and mocked its own video on X.
- **Others:** Burger King, Wendy's and A&W Canada critiqued with joke videos.

**Strategy lesson (S3):** The scripted "product" pitch read as promotion. Joking about its own flop let McDonald's tell a better story.

**Source:** The Takeout, [Rival Fast Food CEOs Make Fun Of McDonald's Big Arch Viral Fumble](https://www.thetakeout.com/2116456/rival-fast-food-ceos-respond-mcdonald-big-arch-viral-video) (Mar 5, 2026)

## Logo Redesign · Flop · Brand target
**True story:** Gap's logo redesign (Oct 2010)

**Target prompt:** "You swapped your classic logo for a new one with no warning. Fans hate it, and someone made a fake account mocking it. Delete, apologize, ignore, or lean in?"
**Others prompt:** "A rival's new logo is getting mocked. Support, critique, or stay out?"

**Learn more:**
- Gap launched the logo without asking anyone, then asked fans on Facebook to design a replacement. Designers saw that as a request for free work.
- The launch arrived alongside a disappointing sales report and a $247M stock drop.
- On this card, **Delete gives no attention**. Quietly switching back gets you nothing.

**What really happened:**
- **Target:** Gap apologized and brought back the old logo within about a week.
- **Others:** the public critiqued it with a mocking fake Twitter account, a "Craplogo" generator and avatar protests.

Brand president Marka Hansen: *"We recognize that we missed the opportunity to engage with the online community."*

**Strategy lesson (S2):** Gap announced the change like a billboard instead of starting a conversation, and only asked its audience for input after the backlash.

**Source:** TechCrunch, [What Did It Take To Get The Gap To Reverse Its Logo Redesign?](https://techcrunch.com/2010/10/11/gap-logo-redesign) (Oct 11, 2010), plus your file *1 - Gap*.

## Launch Ad Backlash · Flop · Brand target · two story variants, one drawn at random

### Variant A: Apple "Crush!" (May 2024)
**Target prompt:** "Your new ad shows a hydraulic press crushing guitars and paint into your tablet. Creative people are furious. Delete, apologize, ignore, or lean in?"
**Others prompt:** "A rival's launch ad is being called anti-creative. Support, critique, or stay out?"

**Learn more:**
- Tim Cook's post got about 19,000 replies, most of them angry.
- Hugh Grant called it "the destruction of the human experience." Paul Graham said "Steve wouldn't have shipped that ad."
- Samsung had just taken back the #1 smartphone spot.

**What really happened:**
- **Target:** Apple apologized: *"We missed the mark with this video, and we're sorry."*
- **Others:** Samsung critiqued with its "UnCrush" ad, tagline "Creativity cannot be crushed."

**Strategy lesson (S1):** Crushing creative tools was the wrong image for an audience of creators.

**Source:** your file *4 apple crush* (Fortune, May 16, 2024); TechCentral, ['Creativity cannot be crushed': Samsung ad mocks Apple](https://techcentral.co.za/creativity-crushed-samsung-ad-mocks-apple/244717/)

### Variant B: Coca-Cola's AI holiday ad (Nov 2024)
**Target prompt:** "You remade your classic holiday ad with AI. People are calling it 'soulless,' and artists are angry. Delete, apologize, ignore, or lean in?"
**Others prompt:** "A rival's AI-made holiday ad is getting slammed by artists. Support, critique, or stay out?"

**Learn more:**
- The video had more than 56 million views. Three AI studios made it using four AI models.
- A marketing professor said the problem was the holiday setting: people link Christmas with Coke and human connection.
- A business professor noted AI ads are much cheaper and predicted companies will keep making them.

**What really happened:**
- **Target:** Coca-Cola didn't pull or apologize for the ad. It defended it as "a collaboration of human storytellers and the power of generative AI," which is closest to Ignore.
- **Others:** *Gravity Falls* creator Alex Hirsch, an influencer, critiqued with a joke: *"FUN FACT: @CocaCola is 'red' because it's made from the blood of out-of-work artists! #HolidayFactz."*

**Strategy lesson (S1, S3):** The format didn't fit the context. A season built on human connection clashed with an AI-made ad.

**Source:** your file *5 - coca cola - holiday*; NBC News, [Coca-Cola causes controversy with AI-made ad](https://www.nbcboston.com/news/national-international/coca-cola-causes-controversy-with-ai-made-ad/3554978/) (Nov 18, 2024)

## Missing Disclosure · Flop · Influencer target
**True story:** Lord & Taylor's paisley dress campaign (2015). The FTC settled with the retailer in March 2016. *New story with no file from you yet. The source is the FTC's own press release.*

**Target prompt:** "Your sponsored post about a dress didn't say it was paid. Followers are noticing, and so is the government. Delete, apologize, ignore, or lean in?"
**Others prompt:** "An influencer got caught posting a paid ad without saying so. Support, critique, or stay out?"

**Learn more:**
- Lord & Taylor gave 50 influencers the dress plus $1,000–$4,000 each. None of the posts said they were paid.
- The posts reached 11.4 million Instagram users in two days, got 328,000 engagements, and the dress sold out. That's a lot of attention, but it rested on hidden ads.
- The FTC charged the **brand**, which matches the card's rule that the contracted brand takes the same trust change.

**What really happened:**
- **Target:** under the settlement, Lord & Taylor must disclose paid endorsements and monitor its influencer campaigns, which is closest to Apologize.
- **Others:** the sources found don't record how other brands or influencers reacted.

**Strategy lesson (S3):** Trust depends on being honest about who's selling. Hidden promotion can win attention while putting trust at risk.

**Source:** FTC, [Lord & Taylor Settles FTC Charges It Deceived Consumers Through Paid Article in an Online Fashion Magazine and Paid Instagram Posts](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2016/03/lord-taylor-settles-ftc-charges-it-deceived-consumers-through-paid-article-online-fashion-magazine) (Mar 15, 2016)

## Copy-Paste Caption · Flop · Influencer target
**True story:** Scott Disick's Bootea post (May 2016)

**Target prompt:** "You posted a sponsored photo, and the brand's instructions went up with it, starting 'Here you go, at 4pm est, write the below.' Screenshots are spreading. Delete, apologize, ignore, or lean in?"
**Others prompt:** "An influencer just posted a brand's script word for word. Support, critique, or stay out?"

**Learn more:**
- The pasted text showed the posting time and the exact caption the brand had written for him.
- Bootea had also worked with Lindsay Lohan, Vanessa Hudgens and Ashley Tisdale, so the slip exposed how scripted celebrity endorsements can be.
- Deleting removes the post but not the screenshots. One was already circulating on Twitter.

**What really happened:**
- **Target:** the caption came down and was replaced, which is closest to Delete. Outlets say the corrected caption added #ad.
- **Others:** Instagram users spotted it fast. Refinery29 called it "an embarrassingly transparent look" at how celebrity endorsements are produced. Naomi Campbell made the same mistake with an Adidas post a month later.

**Strategy lesson (S3):** A scripted brand caption is promotion pretending to be a person, and when the script shows, the trust goes. It's the opposite of Tony Piloseno's "like watching a friend."

**Source:** Refinery29, [Scott Disick Just Made A Super Embarrassing Instagram Mistake](https://www.refinery29.com/2016/05/111399/scott-disick-bootea-instagram) (May 19, 2016). Naomi Campbell: Grazia, [Naomi Campbell made a bit of an Instagram blunder](https://graziadaily.co.uk/celebrity/news/naomi-campbell-made-bit-instagram-blunder/) *(paywalled; not opened)*

## Meme Format · Open · Everyone
**True story:** IHOP becomes "IHOb" (Jun 2018)

**Prompt:** "IHOP says it's now the International House of Burgers, and brands are renaming themselves 'Pancake ___' as a joke. Join the meme (+8 attention, +1 risk) or stay out?"

**Learn more:**
- IHOP had 10 straight quarters of falling sales. The "name change" was a stunt to launch burgers.
- A consultant estimated it earned "several hundred million dollars of free publicity."
- **Every joke is also a free ad for the brand that started it.** Eater called it "the most obnoxious brand move of the year" and begged people to stop tweeting.

**What really happened:**
- **Joined:** Burger King renamed itself "Pancake King," and Wendy's, White Castle, Whataburger and Red Robin all posted jokes.
- **Afterward:** IHOP switched back about a month later.

**Strategy lesson (S2):** The stunt got huge reach. Whether that was worth anything is the spec's third question.

**Source:** your file *3 IHOP* (Eater, Jun 13, 2018); NBC Washington, [Rival Restaurants React to IHOP Name Change](https://www.nbcwashington.com/news/national-international/whatapancake-pancake-castle-rival-restaurants-industry-analysts-react-to-ihop-name-change/2055790/)

## Algorithm Shift · Open · Everyone
**True story:** Snapchat's redesign and Kylie Jenner's tweet (Feb 2018)

**Prompt:** "The platform just changed how feeds work. Brands under contract lose 5 attention unless their influencer matches the trend." The spec gives no decision on this card, so it works as a teachable moment.

**Learn more:**
- Snapchat moved friends and celebrities into separate sections, and more than 1.2 million users petitioned against it.
- CEO Evan Spiegel said of celebrities: *"They're not your friend."*
- Kylie Jenner tweeted *"sooo does anyone else not open Snapchat anymore?"* Snap's stock fell 6% the next day and lost about $1.3B in market value.

**What really happened:** the platform changed under creators and brands, and one influencer's casual post had more effect than the company's statement.

**Strategy lesson (S1):** When the platform changes, what works on it changes too.

**Source:** your PDF, The Guardian, [Kylie Jenner helps to wipe $1bn from Snapchat with tweet over redesign woes](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2018/feb/22/snapchat-redesign-12m-signature-petition-social-media-app-kylie-jenner-celebrities) (Feb 23, 2018)

## Trend Spike · Open · Influencers
**True story:** Sherwin-Williams and paint-mixing TikToker Tony Piloseno (2020–2022)

**Prompt:** "A niche trend is blowing up. Influencers matching this round's trend gain +10 attention." The spec gives no decision on this card.

**Learn more:**
- Tony grew over a million followers mixing paint on TikTok while working part-time at a Sherwin-Williams store. He pitched his ideas to headquarters, was turned down, and was fired.
- He launched his own paint brand, Tonester. By 2023 he had shipped more than 7,000 gallons and was opening a store in Orlando.
- Sherwin-Williams started its own TikTok two years later. An employee said Tony's videos worked because watching them was "like watching a friend instead of him trying to sell you something."

**What really happened:** the brand passed on the creator, and the creator's audience went with him. In game terms, it's an offer a brand should have made. The spec's partnership graph shows offers passed over.

**Strategy lesson (S3, S4):** Authentic creator content beat a brand account that showed up late.

**Source:** your file *2 - Sherwin-Williams*; Business of Home, [After a viral smash, building a paint business, one can at a time](https://businessofhome.com/articles/tony-piloseno-sherwin-williams-tonester-paint) (Aug 10, 2023)
